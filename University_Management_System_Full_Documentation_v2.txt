1. Executive Summary
The University Management System (UMS) is a secure, role-based web application that centralizes academic administration, student lifecycle management, fee collection, examinations, staff administration and student services. It should reduce duplicated data entry, improve the accuracy and timeliness of records, give users self-service access, and provide leadership with reliable operational and financial reports.
1.1 Product objectives
* Create one authoritative student record from application through graduation or withdrawal.
* Support faculties, schools, departments, programmes, courses, academic years, semesters and class groups.
* Digitize admissions, registration, course enrolment, assessment, grading, results approval and transcript requests.
* Manage fees, invoices, receipts, scholarships, sponsorships, refunds and financial reconciliation.
* Give students, lecturers, advisors and administrators role-appropriate dashboards and workflows.
* Track staff profiles, teaching assignments, leave and selected HR functions.
* Provide controlled access to library, hostel, timetable, attendance, notices and student support services.
* Deliver auditable reporting for academic leadership, finance, registry and authorized regulators.
* Protect personal, academic and financial information with least-privilege access, logging and recovery controls.
1.2 Success measures

2. Scope, Assumptions and Exclusions
2.1 In scope
* Public admissions pages and applicant portal.
* Student portal, lecturer portal, advisor access and administrative workspaces.
* Academic structure, curriculum, course offerings, registration and timetable.
* Assessment, grading, results approval and academic progression.
* Student finance, payment-provider integration, receipts and reconciliation.
* Registry services, student documents, transcript and letter requests.
* Staff directory and foundational HR records.
* Library and hostel modules as configurable modules.
* Notifications, reporting, audit logs, configuration and access administration.
2.2 Assumptions
* This specification is for one university, not a public multi-tenant SaaS platform. A future multi-campus deployment can be supported through campus and organizational dimensions.
* The university will nominate process owners and provide approved academic rules, fee schedules, grading scales, document templates and data-retention requirements.
* Payment collection will be delegated to a licensed payment provider. The UMS will not store raw card credentials.
* The university will supply validated legacy data and authorize migration.
* Email/SMS providers, payment gateway credentials, domains and production accounts will be supplied by the institution.
* Identity verification, national ID integrations, regulatory reporting and external student-information exchanges depend on approved APIs and agreements.
2.3 Out of scope for the initial release
* Operating a payment service or holding customer funds independently.
* A complete hospital/clinical records system.
* A full enterprise ERP or full double-entry accounting suite unless separately commissioned.
* Native iOS/Android apps; the initial product is responsive web/PWA-ready.
* Biometric attendance hardware integrations until a vendor and privacy review are approved.
* Automated decisions that replace authorized academic, admissions or disciplinary committees.

3.2 Logical architecture
1. Presentation layer: public site, applicant portal, student portal, lecturer portal and administrative workspace.
2. Application layer: validated use cases, business rules, workflow transitions, report generation and integration adapters.
3. Authorization layer: authenticated identity, role assignments, scoped permissions and institution/campus context.
4. Data layer: PostgreSQL schemas, constraints, indexes, row-level security policies and versioned migrations.
5. Integration layer: payment provider, email/SMS, identity/SSO, learning-management system and accounting exports.
6. Operations layer: audit logs, monitoring, backups, incident response, deployment and recovery.
3.3 Architecture rules
* Keep business logic in explicit service/use-case modules; do not scatter critical rules across UI components.
* Use server-side authorization for every protected operation and RLS as a second enforcement layer.
* Use database transactions for operations that must succeed or fail together, especially enrolment, invoice posting and results publication.
* Use idempotency keys for payment initiation, webhook processing and retryable jobs.
* Use migrations for schema changes; never make undocumented production-only database edits.
* Separate development, staging and production projects, keys, storage buckets and payment credentials.
* Do not expose Supabase service-role keys, payment secrets or signing secrets to the browser.

Users may have more than one role, but privileged roles must be explicitly assigned and reviewed. Use least privilege and scope access by faculty, department, course, campus or assigned student cohort as appropriate.

4.1 Permission design
* Use permissions such as admissions.application.read, admissions.application.review, admissions.decision.approve, academics.course.manage, results.marks.submit, results.publish, finance.invoice.create, finance.refund.approve, registry.transcript.issue and users.roles.manage.
* Store role-to-permission mappings centrally; avoid hard-coded role-name checks throughout the code.
* Support scoped assignments, such as Dean of Faculty A or Lecturer for Course Offering B.
* Require MFA for privileged roles where feasible; enforce re-authentication for high-risk actions.
* Require dual control for refunds above a configurable threshold, result corrections after publication and other high-risk actions.
* Record who granted a role, when it was granted, its scope, expiry (if temporary) and who approved it.
4.2 Separation of duties
The person who creates a payment refund, result amendment, fee waiver or privileged role should not be able to approve the same action when institutional policy requires independent review. Use pending/approved/rejected states and immutable audit events.
5. Functional Modules and Requirements
5.1 Public website and university information
* Display approved information about faculties, departments, programmes, entry requirements, fees, academic calendar, admissions dates, contacts and notices.
* Provide accessible pages, search-engine metadata and mobile-responsive navigation.
* Allow authorized content editors to draft, preview, approve, publish and archive content.
* Keep public content separate from private student and staff records.
5.2 Identity, access and user administration
* Support invitations, registration, email verification, password reset, session revocation and optional MFA.
* Link a login identity to a person record without duplicating student/staff profiles.
* Support account states: invited, pending verification, active, suspended, locked and deactivated.
* Allow authorized administrators to assign roles and scoped permissions with an audit trail.
* Apply rate limits, brute-force protections, secure cookie/session handling and account recovery controls.
* Provide a user activity view to authorized support and security staff.
5.3 Admissions and applicant management
* Configure admission cycles, application periods, applicant categories, programmes, entry requirements, required documents and application fees.
* Provide save-and-resume application forms with server-side validation.
* Accept document uploads into private storage and record file metadata, type, size, uploader and review status.
* Track application statuses: draft, submitted, fee-pending, under-review, incomplete, shortlisted, approved, rejected, waitlisted, offer-issued, offer-accepted, offer-declined and converted-to-student.
* Support reviewer assignment, checklists, comments, scoring/rubrics where approved, recommendation and decision approval.
* Generate application numbers, offer letters and rejection/next-step communications.
* Prevent duplicate submissions where possible and provide controlled merge handling for duplicate applicant records.
* Convert accepted applicants into student records without re-keying data.
5.4 Academic structure and curriculum
* Maintain campuses, faculties/schools, departments, programmes, programme versions, levels/years, course catalogue and prerequisites.
* Configure academic years, semesters/terms, registration periods, add/drop periods and examination periods.
* Define course credits, contact hours, course type, grading scheme, prerequisites, co-requisites and programme requirements.
* Create course offerings by term, section, location, delivery mode and capacity.
* Assign lecturers, teaching assistants and moderators to course offerings.
* Version curriculum rules so historical students retain the programme requirements applicable to their cohort.
* Prevent deletion of referenced academic records; use archival and effective dates.
5.5 Student information system
* Maintain student number, legal/preferred name, contact details, emergency contact, admission pathway, programme, cohort, status and authorized demographic fields.
* Store academic history, enrolments, approved results, holds, progression decisions and graduation status.
* Support student status changes: admitted, active, deferred, leave-of-absence, suspended, withdrawn, completed, graduated and deceased where policy requires.
* Maintain a change history for key fields, including actor, timestamp, reason and prior value where appropriate.
* Support student profile updates with approval for fields that affect official records.
* Provide a consolidated student timeline to authorized staff.
5.6 Registration and enrolment
* Determine registration eligibility based on admission status, fee rules, academic standing, prerequisites, holds and registration dates.
* Allow students to select eligible course offerings subject to capacity, timetable conflicts and credit limits.
* Support add/drop requests, overrides and approval workflows.
* Track enrolment states: selected, pending approval, enrolled, dropped, withdrawn and completed.
* Recalculate registered credits and fee assessments after approved changes.
* Provide clear blocking reasons and a support request path when registration cannot proceed.
* Record manual overrides with reason, authorizer and expiry where applicable.
5.7 Timetable and room scheduling
* Maintain rooms, capacity, equipment, accessibility and campus/location data.
* Schedule lectures, tutorials, laboratories and examinations.
* Detect room, lecturer and cohort clashes before publishing.
* Allow authorized timetable officers to draft, validate, publish and revise schedules.
* Notify affected students and staff of published changes.
* Support timetable views by student, lecturer, room, course and date.
5.8 Attendance
* Record attendance by course session using an authorized lecturer or approved import.
* Support present, absent, late, excused and other institution-defined statuses.
* Allow correction requests and maintain original value, amended value, reason and approver.
* Configure attendance thresholds and alerts where attendance is part of academic policy.
* Restrict students to their own attendance records and lecturers to assigned classes.
5.9 Assessment, grading and results
* Configure assessment components, weights, maximum marks, grading scale, pass rules and rounding policy per course/term.
* Validate that assessment weights and marks are within configured limits.
* Allow lecturers to save drafts, submit marks and respond to moderation requests.
* Support moderation, department review, board approval and controlled publication.
* Lock marks after submission/approval; amendments require reason, approval and audit event.
* Calculate grades, grade points, GPA/CGPA and academic standing using institution-approved rules.
* Support supplementary/resit examinations, incomplete grades, deferred examinations and approved special cases.
* Publish results to eligible students only after the configured approval workflow.
* Generate class lists, result sheets, broadsheets and academic history.
* Provide a controlled grade appeal and review workflow.
5.10 Fees, student finance and payments
* Configure fee categories, programme/level-specific schedules, semester charges, payment deadlines, penalties and approved discounts.
* Generate invoices or account statements from registration and applicable fee rules.
* Support scholarships, sponsorships, waivers, installment plans, credit balances and approved refunds.
* Integrate with a provider-hosted payment flow; store provider references, amounts, currency, status and reconciliation evidence, not raw card data.
* Verify payments server-side and process signed webhooks idempotently.
* Maintain a transaction ledger with pending, successful, failed, reversed, refunded and disputed states.
* Generate receipts only after verified successful payment or approved manual receipt issuance.
* Reconcile provider settlement reports with internal payment records and flag unmatched transactions.
* Apply financial holds according to published policy and provide a clear dispute process.
* Require approval for fee waivers, write-offs and refunds according to thresholds.
5.11 Registry and student services
* Accept requests for transcripts, enrollment letters, completion letters, student ID replacements and certified documents.
* Track request status, fees, supporting documents, assigned officer, SLA and collection/delivery method.
* Generate documents from approved templates with unique reference numbers and verification codes where appropriate.
* Restrict transcript issuance to authorized officers and approved financial/academic conditions.
* Support leave-of-absence, deferral, withdrawal, readmission and change-of-programme requests.
* Maintain an audit trail for issued, reissued, cancelled and corrected official documents.
5.12 Library management
* Manage bibliographic catalogue, physical/digital copies, classifications, locations and availability.
* Manage member eligibility, loans, returns, renewals, reservations, fines and lost/damaged item workflows.
* Support barcode/QR identifiers if equipment is available.
* Send due-date and overdue reminders; support fine waivers with approval.
* Optionally integrate with an existing library management system rather than duplicating its catalogue.
5.13 Hostel and accommodation
* Maintain halls, blocks, rooms, beds, capacity, amenities and availability.
* Configure application windows, eligibility, pricing and allocation rules.
* Support allocation, offer acceptance, payment confirmation, check-in/out, room changes and maintenance requests.
* Prevent double allocation and keep a dated occupancy history.
* Restrict room and welfare information to authorized staff.
5.14 Human resources and staff administration
* Maintain staff profiles, department, job title, employment status, contract dates and emergency contact.
* Track lecturer qualifications and teaching assignments where required.
* Support leave requests, approval chains, leave balances and staff notices.
* Store contracts and confidential HR documents in private storage with restricted access.
* Support payroll export/integration only after local payroll rules and required approvals are defined.
* Keep employee records separate from student records and limit HR access by permission.
5.15 Communications and announcements
* Provide in-app notifications and email/SMS delivery through approved providers.
* Support audience targeting by role, programme, cohort, course, faculty or campus.
* Use templates with variables, language, sender identity and preview.
* Track queued, sent, delivered, bounced and failed status where provider data is available.
* Avoid exposing recipients to each other in bulk email and avoid including sensitive personal information in SMS.
* Provide notification preferences for non-essential messages; retain mandatory operational notices according to policy.
5.16 Student support and case management
* Allow students to open cases for academic advising, IT, finance, welfare, disability support or general inquiries.
* Assign cases to teams, set priority and SLA, record private staff notes and track status.
* Restrict sensitive case categories to specifically authorized teams.
* Allow escalation, reassignment, resolution confirmation and feedback.
* Do not use general-purpose notes for highly sensitive information unless policy, access and retention controls are defined.
5.17 Reports and dashboards
* Provide role-specific dashboards and date/filter controls.
* Support export to CSV/XLSX/PDF with permission checks, row limits and audit logs.
* Generate admissions funnel, enrolment, course capacity, attendance, result, progression, graduation, fee balance, collection, refund, hostel occupancy, library and staff reports.
* Show report generation timestamp, applied filters, data owner and definitions.
* Use server-side pagination and asynchronous export jobs for large datasets.
* Ensure aggregate reports do not inadvertently expose identifiable student or staff data.
5.18 System configuration and audit
* Manage university profile, campuses, academic calendar, grading scales, fee rules, numbering formats, templates, notification channels and maintenance notices.
* Keep high-risk settings behind explicit permissions and, where needed, approval.
* Record sign-ins, role changes, record access for sensitive areas, exports, result publication, fee changes, refunds and administrative overrides.
* Provide searchable audit filters and restricted export for authorized auditors.
* Never permit ordinary administrators to edit or delete audit events.
6. Key End-to-End Workflows
6.1 Applicant to enrolled student
7. Admissions administrator configures cycle, programmes, entry rules, required documents and application fee.
8. Applicant creates an account, verifies email and completes a saveable application.
9. Applicant uploads documents to private storage and submits the application.
10. System validates required fields and payment status where an application fee applies.
11. Admissions staff review the application and record recommendations; designated approver makes the decision.
12. System sends the decision and, if approved, issues an offer with acceptance deadline and conditions.
13. Applicant accepts the offer and completes any required onboarding steps.
14. System creates or links the student profile, assigns student number, programme and cohort, and records the conversion event.
15. Student completes registration and receives a course timetable and fee statement.
6.2 Course registration
16. Student signs in and opens the active registration period.
17. System checks status, holds, prerequisites, course availability, credit limits and timetable clashes.
18. Student selects courses; the system reserves capacity transactionally or rechecks capacity at final confirmation.
19. Any override routes to the designated approver.
20. On confirmation, enrolments are committed and applicable charges are assessed.
21. Student sees confirmed courses, credits, timetable and account balance.
6.3 Results approval and publication
22. Lecturer enters assessment marks and submits the mark sheet.
23. System validates missing marks, weight totals, grade ranges and course assignment.
24. Moderator or department reviewer checks the submission and requests corrections or recommends approval.
25. Authorized board/academic officer approves results according to policy.
26. System locks the approved version and records the approver and timestamp.
27. Results are published to eligible students and notifications are queued.
28. Any post-publication change requires a formal amendment request, independent approval and preserved history.
6.4 Payment and reconciliation
29. System creates an invoice/account item and a payment attempt with a unique internal reference.
30. Student is redirected to or opens the provider's secure checkout.
31. Provider sends the user back to the application; the return page alone does not mark payment successful.
32. Server verifies provider status or processes an authenticated/signed webhook.
33. Webhook handler validates signature, amount, currency, reference and transaction state, then processes idempotently.
34. System records the verified payment and allocates it to the invoice in a database transaction.
35. Receipt becomes available after success is confirmed.
36. Finance reconciles provider settlement reports and resolves unmatched or reversed transactions.
6.5 Transcript request
37. Student submits request, selects delivery method and pays any applicable approved fee.
38. System checks identity, academic record status, holds and required approvals.
39. Registry officer reviews and approves issuance.
40. System generates the transcript from approved records using the official template and a unique document reference.
41. The document is securely delivered or made available through a short-lived authenticated link.
42. System logs issuance, download or reissue events as required by policy.

8. Data Model and Database Design
8.1 Core design principles
* Use UUID primary keys for internal records and human-readable unique numbers for student, applicant, invoice and document references.
* Use foreign keys, unique constraints, check constraints and indexes for integrity; do not rely only on application code.
* Store timestamps in UTC and display in the configured university timezone.
* Use numeric/decimal types for money and explicitly store currency codes.
* Use effective dates or versioned records for programme, fee and grading rules that change over time.
* Use soft archival for records that must be retained; do not hard-delete official academic or financial history.
* Keep private files outside public buckets; store metadata and access policy in the database.
* Define data retention, anonymization and deletion processes consistent with applicable law and institutional policy.

8.3 Important constraints
* Unique student_number, applicant_number, invoice_number and document_reference within their intended scope.
* A student cannot enrol twice in the same course offering unless an explicitly approved repeat rule allows it.
* Course offering capacity must not be exceeded; final enrolment requires a transactional capacity check.
* Assessment component weights must satisfy the configured grading policy.
* Marks must fall within allowed ranges and approved grade rules.
* Posted invoice/payment records and approved results must not be silently overwritten.
* Provider transaction references and webhook event IDs must be unique to prevent duplicate processing.
* A successful payment allocation cannot exceed the unallocated payment amount or invoice balance unless overpayment rules explicitly allow a credit.
* Only one active bed allocation may exist for a bed at a given time.
* Role assignments must be checked for scope and expiry on every protected request.
8.4 Data lifecycle and migration
43. Inventory source systems, spreadsheets, paper records and document repositories.
44. Define a data dictionary, ownership, authoritative source and cleansing rules.
45. Map legacy identifiers to new UUIDs and preserve original student numbers where required.
46. Run trial migrations in staging and reconcile record counts, financial totals, grades and enrolments.
47. Obtain business-owner sign-off before production import.
48. Freeze source changes or define a controlled delta migration during cutover.
49. Retain migration logs and a rollback/recovery plan; do not expose unverified migrated results.
9. API and Integration Standards
9.1 API conventions
* Use versioned REST endpoints or server actions with clear service boundaries; use REST for external integrations.
* Validate all input with Zod on the server and return consistent error shapes.
* Use cursor pagination or bounded page-based pagination for large lists.
* Use ISO 8601 timestamps, explicit currency codes and decimal-safe monetary representations.
* Return stable machine-readable error codes without leaking stack traces or sensitive database details.
* Require authentication, authorization, rate limiting and audit logging for protected endpoints.
* Use idempotency keys for actions that create financial or official records.

10. Security, Privacy and Audit
10.1 Baseline controls
* Enforce HTTPS in all environments except isolated local development.
* Use Supabase Auth with secure session handling, verified email and MFA for privileged accounts where feasible.
* Apply least-privilege RBAC and RLS to every table containing private data.
* Review every RLS policy with positive and negative tests; never assume that hiding a UI button prevents access.
* Keep service-role keys and provider secrets server-only and rotate them through a documented procedure.
* Use private storage buckets, file type/size limits, malware scanning where available and short-lived signed URLs.
* Protect against injection, XSS, CSRF where applicable, insecure direct object references, brute force, SSRF and mass assignment.
* Use rate limits for authentication, application submission, exports, payments and public endpoints.
* Log security-relevant events without logging passwords, tokens, full payment secrets or unnecessary sensitive fields.
* Use dependency scanning, secret scanning, code review and security checks in CI.
10.2 Privacy requirements
* Collect only personal data required for a defined university purpose.
* Provide privacy notices explaining purposes, sharing, retention, rights and contact channels.
* Define retention and disposal schedules for applications, student records, staff records, finance records and support cases.
* Restrict sensitive categories and exports; use masking or aggregation where possible.
* Define processes for data access/correction requests, incident handling and vendor review.
* Assess applicable Ghanaian data-protection requirements and any cross-border hosting or transfer obligations with qualified counsel.
10.3 Audit event fields
At minimum, an audit event should capture: event ID, timestamp, actor identity, actor role/scope at the time, action, entity type, entity ID, outcome, request/correlation ID, source IP/user-agent where lawful and useful, reason/approval reference, and a minimal before/after summary for approved sensitive fields. Protect audit storage from ordinary updates and deletions.
10.4 High-risk actions
* Role assignment or privilege escalation
* Publication or amendment of official results
* Student status changes that affect academic standing
* Fee schedule changes, waivers, write-offs and refunds
* Payment reconciliation adjustments
* Transcript issuance and reissuance
* Bulk data export and sensitive document access
* Changes to RLS policies, integration secrets or security settings

14. Testing and Acceptance Criteria
14.1 Testing strategy
* Unit tests for grading, GPA/CGPA, fee assessment, eligibility, discounts, refund rules and permission helpers.
* Integration tests for database constraints, RLS, payment webhooks, email jobs and migration scripts.
* End-to-end tests for application submission, admission approval, registration, payment, results publication and transcript request.
* Role-based negative tests to prove that users cannot read or mutate another user's records without permission.
* Concurrency tests for course capacity, duplicate payment webhooks and simultaneous invoice allocation.
* Accessibility tests for forms, keyboard navigation, error messages and modal workflows.
* Load tests for peak registration, results release and bulk notifications.
* Backup restoration and incident-response exercises before go-live.
14.2 Minimum acceptance criteria
50. An applicant can submit a complete application and see its status without accessing another applicant's record.
51. Admissions staff can review applications, and only authorized approvers can issue final decisions.

53. Registration enforces prerequisites, date windows, credit limits, holds, clashes and course capacity.

62. Critical automated tests pass in CI before production release.

15. Deployment, Operations and Disaster Recovery
15.1 Environments
* Local development: synthetic data only; no production secrets.
* Preview/staging: isolated database and storage; test payment credentials; representative but sanitized data.
* Production: separate project, least-privilege keys, restricted access, monitoring and controlled deployments.
15.2 Release process
64. Developer creates a branch and pull request.
65. CI runs linting, TypeScript checks, unit tests, integration tests and secret/dependency scans.
66. Database migrations are reviewed and tested against a staging copy.
67. Product owner and module owner approve the release notes and any policy changes.
68. Deploy to staging and run smoke tests plus critical end-to-end journeys.
69. Apply a reviewed production migration using a backward-compatible approach where possible.
70. Deploy the application, verify health checks and monitor error/payment/job dashboards.
71. Maintain a documented rollback or forward-fix plan; never assume database changes can always be reversed safely.
15.3 Backup and recovery
* Confirm Supabase plan-specific backup and point-in-time recovery capabilities before committing to an RPO.
* Keep independent exports or backups where required by institutional policy and test restoration, not just backup creation.
* Define RPO (maximum tolerable data loss) and RTO (maximum tolerable service interruption) with leadership.
* Document who can initiate recovery, how integrity is checked and how users are informed.
* Reconcile financial and academic transactions after recovery and investigate any missing or duplicated events.
15.4 Monitoring and incident response
* Alert on application error spikes, failed authentication patterns, webhook signature failures, stuck background jobs, payment reconciliation gaps and storage/DB capacity warnings.
* Use correlation IDs to trace a workflow across the web app, database, jobs and providers.
* Maintain an incident runbook with severity, escalation contacts, evidence preservation, containment, recovery and post-incident review.
* Review privileged access periodically and revoke accounts promptly when staff leave or change roles.

Appendix A. Recommended Build Principles
* Build the data model and authorization policies before polishing dashboards.
* Make workflows explicit state machines rather than loosely related booleans.
* Treat student records, approved results and financial postings as auditable records.
* Never trust client-side role checks, payment redirects or hidden form fields.
* Prefer database constraints and transactions for invariants that must always hold.
* Keep provider-specific integrations behind adapters so providers can be replaced.
* Use feature flags for staged rollout of optional modules.
* Use seeded synthetic data for demos and automated tests; do not copy real student records into local development.
* Document operational procedures as well as code: access reviews, refunds, grade corrections, data exports, incident response and recovery.

D.3 Required implementation conventions
* Use TypeScript strict mode and keep shared domain types close to the modules that own them.
* Use Supabase Auth with @supabase/ssr cookie-based clients for Next.js server rendering; create separate browser and server client helpers.
* Use RLS for private tables and storage access. Test policies as each role and as an unauthenticated user. A UI permission check is not a security boundary.
* Use server-side Zod validation for all writes, even when client validation is present.
* Use PostgreSQL NUMERIC for monetary values and Decimal.js for calculations that occur in TypeScript. Define currency and rounding rules explicitly.
* Use migrations in source control, reviewed pull requests, staging verification and controlled production releases.
* Use background jobs for retryable, long-running tasks such as bulk notifications and report exports; keep jobs idempotent.
* Do not expose service-role keys, webhook secrets or provider private keys in browser bundles, public environment variables or source control.
* Use a hosted payment checkout or provider tokenization; never store raw card numbers or CVV.
* Treat all provider webhooks as untrusted until signature, reference, amount, currency and status have been verified.
